Privacy Policy — Yunlo

Last updated: 16 October 2026

Yunlo teaches tai chi walking. It has no accounts and no sign-in, and what it keeps about your walks stays on your phone. Yunlo is free and shows ads, and the ad provider, Google, collects some data to serve them. This policy says what is stored, what leaves your device, and who to contact. It is written to be accurate rather than reassuring.

What Yunlo keeps, on your phone

Your settings (minutes a day, weekly goal, whether the reminder is on and its time, text size, theme, keeping the screen awake, the voice and its speed, the chime, the view a walk opens in, haptics, whether the body log and the energy line are on, and your weight and waist units), your answers to the safety check, your place in the plan, the steps and movements you marked as known, a log of your walks (when, which walk and for how many minutes), the weight and waist entries you type in if you turn the body log on, and whether you have seen the “Drag to turn” hint. It also keeps two small counts used only to space out full-screen ads: how many times you have tapped Done after a walk, and how many times the app has been opened today. All of this is stored only in the app’s own storage on your device. None of it is sent to us, and none of it is given to the ad provider.

Backups

On Android, Yunlo turns the system’s app backup off, so none of this is copied into your device backup: reinstalling Yunlo or setting up a new phone starts it fresh. On iPhone and iPad, the app’s storage is part of your device backup if you make one; that backup is kept in your own iCloud account or on your own computer, not by us.

Health information

The safety check asks whether a few health situations apply to you, so the app can suggest checking with a clinician first. The body log, off unless you turn it on, keeps the weight and waist measurements you type in, and the optional energy line on the “Walk done” screen is worked out on the phone from your latest weight. None of this ever leaves your phone: it is not sent to us or to the ad provider, and it is never used to choose ads. Yunlo does not connect to Health Connect, Google Fit, Apple Health or any wearable, and reads no health data from your device. Yunlo offers general guidance on gentle exercise. It is not a medical device and not medical advice.

What Yunlo does not collect

Yunlo does not collect your name, email address, precise location, contacts or photos. It does not use your camera or microphone. It contains no analytics or crash-reporting tool of its own: we receive no reports about how you use the app. The only data that leaves your device is described under Advertising.

Advertising

Yunlo shows ads supplied by Google AdMob: small banners at the end of screens, plus one at the top of the walk screen that you can close; a full-screen ad on every second time you tap Done after a walk; and a full-screen ad on every third time you open the app in a day. To serve those ads, Google’s advertising software collects and processes your advertising ID and other device identifiers Google uses for ads; your IP address, from which a general, approximate location can be estimated; how you interact with ads and the app, such as the app being opened and an ad being shown or tapped; and diagnostic information about how the app and the ad software perform. Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, and acts as an independent controller of it. What Google does with it is governed by Google’s own policies: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites and policies.google.com/privacy. Yunlo has no in-app purchases and no paid way to remove the ads.

When the ad software starts

Yunlo starts Google’s advertising software only once the consent step described below is done, never before it, and it holds back the software’s own measurement until then. Until that point nothing is sent to Google for ads apart from the consent check itself.

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

Before Yunlo requests any ad, it checks with Google’s consent service. Where your answer is needed, it shows you Google’s consent form, which asks whether ads may use your data, and waits for your answer. Your answer is stored on your device by Google’s consent tool and sent with ad requests, so that Google can honour it. You can change your answer at any time in the app under You, then About, then Ad privacy choices, which reopens the same form.

On iPhone and iPad

Yunlo asks only for non-personalised ads on iPhone and iPad, and it never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, so your device’s advertising identifier is not used to track you across other companies’ apps and websites.

Reminders

If you turn on the daily reminder, Yunlo asks your phone for permission to show notifications and schedules them on the phone itself. No server is involved. You can turn the reminder off in You, under Your walks, or in your phone’s settings.

Voice instructions

The spoken instructions use your phone’s own text-to-speech. Yunlo gives it only the instructions to read out, nothing about you, and does not record or send anything you say.

Links out of the app

Where the app offers Rate Yunlo, it opens our page in the store you installed from. Share Yunlo opens your phone’s own share sheet. The link to notification settings opens your phone’s settings. Yunlo sends nothing to those pages.

Permissions

On Android these appear as declared permissions: internet access and network state, for ads; the advertising ID and Android’s ad-related Privacy Sandbox permissions, for ads; keeping the device awake briefly, for the ad software’s background tasks; notifications, asked only if you turn the reminder on, with restoring your reminder after the phone restarts; and vibration, for haptic feedback you can turn off in You. Keeping the screen on during a walk needs no permission. Yunlo asks for no location, camera, microphone, contacts or storage access. On iPhone and iPad the app asks only for notifications, and only if you turn the reminder on.

Children

Yunlo is meant for a general audience aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact greysonmiller67@gmail.com and we will act on it.

Erasing what Yunlo keeps

You can erase it yourself, inside the app, at any time. Under You, Delete all my data erases everything Yunlo keeps on your phone at once (your walk log, your place in the plan, the steps and movements you marked as known, your weight and waist entries, your settings and reminder, your safety answers, and the two ad counts) and the app starts again from its welcome screen. Reset progress, in the same place, clears only your walk log, your place in the plan and the steps and movements you marked as known, and keeps your settings, your safety answers and your weight and waist entries. Turning the body log off offers to delete every weight and waist entry. None of these can be undone. None of them changes your advertising ID or the answer you gave to the ad consent form: those are kept by your device and by Google’s ad software, and you change them as described under Your rights.

Data we hold about you

None. We operate no servers and have no database. Because the app has no account, there is nothing for us to look up, export or delete: Delete all my data, uninstalling Yunlo, or clearing its storage in your phone’s settings, each delete everything it kept on the device. Requests about data held by Google for advertising must be made to Google, using the links above and your device’s ad settings, because we have no access to it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or restrict the use of personal data about you, and to object to it being used for advertising. Since we hold no personal data ourselves, you exercise them like this. For advertising: through the consent form in the app, under You, then About, then Ad privacy choices, where it applies, and through your device’s ad settings (on Android, Settings, then Google, then Ads; on iPhone and iPad, Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Tracking, and Apple Advertising), where you can reset or delete your advertising ID. For everything stored in the app: under You, then Delete all my data, or by deleting the app or clearing its storage.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will appear here with a new date.
